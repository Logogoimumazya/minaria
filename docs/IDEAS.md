# Ideas and references

A running list, added to every round: references from builders and history, ideas from other games' lore, and
what each would mean for Minaria. Items move to a task in `AUTOPILOT.md` (or `TOWN_KIT.md` §4) when taken up.

## References

- **Medieval open fields** ([Internet Archaeology 19](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue19/5/5.2.cfm),
  [Open field system](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/117737),
  [Ridge and furrow](https://www.fieldworthy.co.uk/guides/ridge-and-furrow-explained)):
  a village farmed two or three great fields, each a group of furlongs, each furlong cut into strips
  (selions ~7-9 yards wide, up to 220 yards long) separated by grass balks, with headlands where the plough
  turned. One great field lay fallow each year. Beyond them: common pasture, woodland (pigs, timber), a green.
- **King's Landing (WesterosCraft)** ([Planet Minecraft](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/showcase---kings-landing-an-epic-city-1843386/)):
  the standard; its outskirts are measured in `OUTSKIRTS.md`.
- Other large city builds to look at for the grand-city proposal (task 8): Minecraft Middle-Earth (Minas
  Tirith, Hobbiton), Stone_Golem's
  [Realistic Medieval City](https://www.9minecraft.net/realistic-medieval-city-map/), the Warhammer "Imperial
  City" project. Licences to be checked one by one before anything is used.

## Ideas for the country (task 3 follow-ups)

1. **Great fields and rotation.** Group a village's furlongs into 2-3 great fields; all furlongs of one field
   share a state: ripe wheat (age 6-7), young crop (age 1-3, green), or fallow (grass, sheep). Reads as a
   patchwork from afar, as a real open-field village does.
2. **Headlands.** A 3-4 block grass strip across the ends of the strips of a furlong (where the plough turns),
   and ridge-and-furrow on slopes: alternate strips raised by a slab.
3. **Common and green.** Every village gets a green (grass, a big tree, a well or cross) and a common pasture
   with sheep or cows at the field edge; hamlets share one.
4. **Mills.** A watermill where a lane crosses a stream within reach of the fields; a windmill on the highest
   ground of the field belt of towns without a stream.
5. **Woodland pasture.** Pigs and swineherd huts in the wood belt; charcoal burners' mounds (smoke) deeper in.
6. **Wayside.** Crosses or shrines at lane junctions (the Church's states more often), milestones on the gate
   roads, gallows outside capitals.

## Ideas from other games' lore

- **Kingdom Come: Deliverance (Rattay):** trades that stink or need water sit downstream and outside the wall:
  tanners, dyers, butchers' yards by the river below the town. Minaria: put tanneries and dye-works on the river
  downstream of each town (and name the lane after them).
- **Mount & Blade:** every castle has villages bound to it; the villages' look follows the castle's culture.
  Minaria: a castle town's hamlets take the castle's state culture (walls.CULTURES) for their stone and roofs.
- **Total War (provinces):** a province is a capital with minor settlements, each with one speciality (mine,
  port, farmland, timber). Minaria: give each village one speciality from its terrain (fishing on shores, timber
  in forest biomes, quarry near rock, vineyard on warm slopes) and build the matching outskirts piece.
- **Dark Souls / Elden Ring (decaying empire):** Austrum's country near the Wound should show decline: fallow
  and overgrown fields, roofless farmsteads, a watchtower in ruin — the contrast with prosperous Valaria across
  the border tells the story without words.
